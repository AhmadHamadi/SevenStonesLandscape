Sod Installation in Hamilton

By Riaad, Owner & Project Manager · ICPI Certified Installer · Last updated July 28, 2026

A lot of Hamilton lawns are fighting the same thing: thin, tired turf sitting on compacted clay or the skim of topsoil a builder left behind. From the Mountain brow down to Corktown and out toward the Ancaster edge, we strip the old lawn, fix the grade, and lay fresh sod on a proper depth of screened topsoil. Done right in spring or early fall, you get a thick lawn that roots in instead of browning out by August.

Why Hamilton Homeowners Resod

Most people call us because the lawn they have is losing. Older lots around Durand and Corktown often carry decades of compacted soil under the grass, and newer builds on the Mountain frequently sit on a thin layer of topsoil over graded clay fill. Grass roots have nowhere to go, so the lawn thins, weeds move in, and no amount of seed seems to fix it.

What actually makes sod succeed here is the prep, not the sod itself. The Halton Till clay across much of the lower city and the Mountain drains slowly and packs down hard, so we grade the lot to move water away from the foundation and build a proper root zone with four to six inches of quality screened topsoil. Near Cootes Paradise and parts of Westdale the soil runs sandier, which drains faster but dries out quicker, so watering matters even more early on.

The other half is timing and water. Sod laid in late spring or early fall, then watered daily for the first two to three weeks, roots in cleanly. Sod dropped in July heat with a light sprinkle browns at the seams. Get the grade, the topsoil, and the watering right and a Hamilton lawn fills in thick within a growing season.

Local Considerations & Credentials

  • Certified and insured: ICPI certified installer and Landscape Ontario member, established in 2013, with $5M liability insurance, full WSIB coverage, and a 5-year workmanship warranty on every install.
  • Hamilton soil and shade: Halton Till clay dominates the lower city and the Mountain, with sandier ground near Cootes Paradise and Westdale. Mature trees in Durand and Westdale throw heavy shade, so we match the sod blend to the spot.
  • Real prep, not a shortcut: We strip the old lawn, regrade so water runs away from the house, and lay four to six inches of screened topsoil before any sod goes down. That root zone is what keeps the lawn alive.
  • Riaad's Pro Tip: Book your sod for late April to June or September to mid October. If you must go in summer, plan to water twice a day for the first two weeks, or the seams will dry and pull apart before the roots grab.

Getting Sod Right in Hamilton

Hamilton covers a huge spread of neighbourhoods and soil, so no two sod jobs are the same. A century home in Durand might have compacted clay and old buried debris under the grass, while a newer Mountain build often has thin topsoil over graded fill. We check what is actually under your lawn before quoting, because that is what decides how much topsoil and grading the job needs.

Timing and watering are the parts homeowners control after we leave. Aim for a spring or early fall install, then commit to daily watering for the first two to three weeks while the roots knit into the new soil. Hold off on heavy foot traffic and the first mow until the sod is rooted. Do that and the lawn will fully establish over the season instead of struggling.

How We Scope Hamilton Sod Projects

Scoping starts with a site visit. We measure the area, check how the lot drains, and dig into what is under the existing lawn, whether that is Halton Till clay, sandier soil near Cootes, or a thin builder skim over fill. We look at shade from mature trees, low spots where water pools, and how the grade sits against the foundation. That tells us how much old lawn to strip, how much topsoil to bring in, and how much grading the job really needs.

From there we give you a clear per-square-foot price with the prep spelled out, no vague allowances. We confirm the right sod blend for your sun and shade, book into a spring or early fall window when we can, and walk you through the watering schedule before we start. You know what is included, what it costs, and what the lawn needs from you in those first few weeks.
