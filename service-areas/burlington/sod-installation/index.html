Why Burlington Homeowners Resod and What Makes It Work Here

Most people call us because the old lawn gave up. Years of clay compaction, thin patchy grass, drainage that never quite worked, or a lot the builder finished with barely an inch of topsoil. In the older established neighbourhoods off New Street and Guelph Line, we often see lawns worn down under mature shade. Up in the newer north Burlington subdivisions it is almost always thin fill over compacted subsoil. Either way, patching does not fix it. A clean resod does.

What separates a Burlington lawn that thrives from one that limps along is the prep nobody sees. Clay holds water, so if the grade is off, you get soggy low spots and runoff pooling near the foundation. We shape every lot to slope gently away from the house first. Then comes four to six inches of quality screened topsoil, which is the layer roots actually live in. Skip either step and the best sod in the province will still struggle.

Timing matters too. We lay most Burlington sod in spring, from late April into June, or in early fall between September and mid-October, when the soil is warm but the air has cooled off. That is when new grass knits down fastest. We can sod through summer, but only if you are ready to water it hard every day while it roots.

Local Guidance for a Lawn That Lasts in Burlington

The single biggest mistake we see on Burlington lawns is skimping on topsoil. A lot of resods go down on two inches of soil over hard clay, and the grass looks fine for a season before it thins out and browns in the heat. Roots need room. Four to six inches of screened topsoil gives them somewhere to go, holds moisture through a dry July, and keeps the lawn from baking down to the subsoil. It is the least glamorous part of the job and the part that decides everything.

Drainage is the other thing to get right before the sod ever arrives. Burlington clay does not shed water quickly, so a lot that slopes the wrong way will pond against the foundation and drown low corners of the lawn. We check the grade across the whole property, not just the patch getting sod, and correct the fall so runoff heads to the street or a swale. On lakefront and escarpment-edge lots this takes more shaping, and it is worth every bit.

How We Scope Burlington Sod Projects

When we scope a Burlington sod project, we start at the lot itself. We look at how the ground slopes, where water sits after rain, how much shade the mature trees throw, and what is under the existing lawn. On an older Aldershot or downtown property that usually means dealing with compacted clay and tree roots. On a newer north-end build it means measuring how thin the builder topsoil really is. We measure the area, flag the low spots, and figure out how much old turf needs to come off.

From there we build a quote that spells out the grading, the topsoil depth, the sod blend, and any haul-away. We tell you which install window fits your timeline and what the watering commitment looks like once the sod is down. No vague lump sums. You see the price per square foot, the prep included, and where the numbers can move. Then we book the crew, Monday through Saturday, and get your new lawn rooted.
